The Loom

The Ministry knows what incomes would look like with its programme, and without it. It knows the average gain. Now it wants to know whose lives would improve.

The two columns are exact income distributions. Each thread joins one possible life without the programme to that same person’s possible life with it. The threads are hypotheses. The records do not tell you how to join them.

Reweave the lives without changing the evidence. Find the smallest and largest shares that could satisfy each commission, keep a weave for each, and publish your bounds.

Sometimes the share is fully identified. Sometimes a range is the strongest possible conclusion. Both are a result worth publishing.

How to weave

Change a possible world. Tap two right endpoints to exchange their threads, or drag one onto another. Keyboard users can tab to the endpoints and press Enter or Space. Every endpoint is used once.

Read the threads. The number beside a thread is its income change: with minus without. Green threads gain, grey threads tie, red threads lose. The commission may ask about gains, losses, or gains of a particular size.

Keep two witnesses. Save a weave with as few qualifying people as possible, then one with as many as possible. These snapshots remain fixed while you experiment. Restore one to continue working on it.

Publish. Both bounds must be attainable and sharp. A referee checks all admissible pairings. After an unsuccessful submission, you can keep trying or ask to see a counterexample; using one is marked in your record.

Respect the brief. District labels are additional observed information. No harm and preserved ranks are assumptions, explicitly marked as such. The board rejects swaps that violate them.

The records are exact population frequencies, not a small random sample. Each of the n threads carries 1/n of the population. The game isolates identification from sampling error. No secret “true” weave is drawn.
