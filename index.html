    For each group, the gain from both programmes is training alone + transport alone + extra effect. A partner reports the weighted average of the difference between your two versions. Use its recruitment percentages as weights.

    Welcome

    What makes the programmes work?

    Training and transport may raise income differently for different people. Choose pilots, spend your research budget, and separate their effects—or show why you cannot.

    Start by choosing a partner and two programme versions. Add the pilot to your plan, then run it.

    How to play

    What are you trying to find?

    How much training alone changes weekly income, how much transport alone changes it, and the extra gain or loss from using both together. Effects can vary across people. Your mission states which differences are allowed.

    If training adds 2, transport adds 3 and the extra effect is −1, the combined package adds 4. These are just example numbers. An extra effect can be positive, zero or negative.

    What can you choose?

    1. Select one of seven recruitment partners. The four coloured cells show who it recruits.
    2. Click a reference programme card, then the version to compare with it. Some partners cannot offer every version.
    3. Add pilots to your free plan. Check the total cost, then run the plan or just its first pilot.

    Results appear in the notebook. A result is comparison version minus reference version. All pilots use random assignment with the same group proportions in both arms.

    What information do pilots provide?

    One exact, pooled average difference. Partners disclose recruitment shares but cannot link individual outcomes to group labels in their reports. You cannot request separate group results or change a partner’s mix. Each contract funds a two-arm pilot; a new comparison uses new arms. The simulation removes sampling error to focus on identification.

    Programme effects are stable across partners and pilots for the same group. Baseline incomes may differ between pilots, so only within-pilot comparisons are valid. Repeating the same comparison adds no information and is unavailable.

    How do you enter an answer?

    Open Results & report. Choose Give the effects, then enter every requested effect. Numbers, negative values and fractions such as 3/2 are accepted. Give values to the nearest 0.01 income unit. The calculator fills one model that matches the evidence, but it does not tell you whether that model is unique.

    Alternatively, choose Show why impossible. Enter changes to the effects that would leave every permitted pilot result unchanged. Use at least one nonzero change. This exhibits two different effect patterns that the entire research network cannot distinguish. Simply exhausting your budget does not establish impossibility.

    How are scores calculated?

    A sealed false or unsupported report scores zero and ends the round. A numerical report needs enough evidence to determine all its effects uniquely; even a lucky correct guess is insufficient. A valid impossibility proof is checked against all permitted pilots, including ones you did not run.

    For a correct report, score = round[100 × (optimal cost + 1) / (your spending + 1)]. The game computes the exact cheapest design, allowing for opening fees and existing results. The maximum is 100. A valid impossibility proof costs no experiments, so its benchmark is zero. The +1 makes that case well defined.

    Replays change the income effects. New contract also changes prices and recalculates the budget and optimum. These scores are tracked separately from the authored missions. There is no penalty for taking time or revising a free plan.
